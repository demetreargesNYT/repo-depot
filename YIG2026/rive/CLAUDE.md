## Rooms (the rule for all fine-tuning)

**What is in Rive now is the current version of every room.** The scene file (`chapters/chN/scene.rml`) is the only source for a room's intro, its ball and its loop; there is no other version to match, and any change you make becomes the newest version of it. Demetre tunes each room on its own, so:

- **Room-by-room calls, decided during finesse:** how the ball enters (it won't be the same on every card, and sometimes it is just part of the loop), whether it squashes, whether a room gets a full ball journey or keeps a hop, and where a loop starts. Nothing makes these uniform across rooms.
- **Never re-run `tools/import_room.py` on a room that is already in unless he asks.** It would overwrite his edits. The configs in `tools/rooms/` record how each room first came in; they are history and go stale as soon as a room is tuned. Do not "sync" them.
- **His hand edits in the Rive Editor come in with `rive pull`, then a commit.** Before touching a room, pull into a temp folder (or `rive pull` in the chapter's folder after he says he has edited) and compare structure, not bytes; commit his pull before making any change of your own.
- **Changes he asks for are made by editing that room's keys directly** in `scene.rml` (the intro, ball or loop animation of that one room), then `npm run finesse <ch> "<room>"`. To see what a change did, `python3 tools/compare_room.py <ch> <scene>` renders the room now and at the last commit. A key's interpolation shapes the segment that leaves it. Give new interpolators ids no other element uses.
- **A change in one room never touches another.** Every room has its own intro, Waiting pose, ball keys and loop; no piece is keyed by two rooms. `npm run check` enforces it (each room's timelines may key only pieces inside that room; the `Colors` poses key only the shared backdrop). The things all rooms share on purpose are the backdrop's wall, floor and floor line (crossfaded by each room's own `Colors` pose), the Top Marker for draw order, the `scene`, `direction` and `lockup` properties, and the copy timing (the first-visit constant, 2.0 s, or 3.2 s with a Chapter 2 lockup).
- **Keep the room sheets current:** run `python3 tools/rooms_sheet.py <ch>` after any room change (`docs/rooms.md` for ch2, `docs/rooms-ch1.md`, `docs/rooms-ch3.md`). `npm run check` fails when one is out of date. He uses them to ask for changes like "The Mini: Move 2 of the ball 0.1 s slower". Each room's "To tune later" text comes from the chapter's `chapter.json` (`tuneLater`; ch2's note is in `tools/rooms_sheet.py`), so edit it there.

## Motion rules

- **Before you touch the ball, read `docs/ball-character.md`** (who the ball is, its beats with measured timings, the timing laws, the don'ts, the PARKED check).
- **Before you touch a cube, read `docs/cube-grammar.md`** (the grid, the named moves with their timings, the laws, scale across the crossword family, the cube engine).
- One Rive file per chapter, to keep file sizes down. The artboard is 1495 × 1067 and serves both desktop and mobile. Balls and key pieces stay inside the safe area, x 560 to 935 and y 0 to 812 (the Figma phone frame); engineering's framing decides the actual crop.
- The transition between rooms is Roll and pan. **Next moves the camera right; Back is its exact mirror**, moving it left:
  - The outgoing room leaves left. Its wall plane nets 1280 units of travel and its floor plane 1600.
  - The incoming room arrives on the same pan. Rooms carry art only.
  - **A room never freezes mid-motion.** When it pans out on Next, its loop and its small loops keep playing as it leaves. A room that is already built pans back in already moving on Back. Any exception is a one-off, listed with its room (none: the global pass surveyed every room, and `npm run check` fails a room whose Intro, Hold or Loop state can be left on a scene change; `tools/loop_motion.py <ch>`, part of the suite, plays the pan out and back in and compares every piece with a run that never leaves).
  - **Intended, not a freeze:** on a room's first visit the room pans in and plays its intro, and its small loops (the "<Room> Small" layers) hold their opening pose until the pan ends (1.65 s), then start (Demetre, 2026-10-30).
- **Color changes by crossfade, never by a seam.** One shared backdrop (wall, floor, floor line) stays put under every room, with no strokes on its edges, so no hard edge ever crosses the stage. On a scene change its wall and floor colors fade to the new room's over 1.45 s, power1.inOut, starting with the tap; a tap mid-fade turns from the color on screen.
  - Everything that leaves clears the full artboard, and everything that arrives starts outside it.
- The red ball is the persistent device:
  - The outgoing room's ball is never keyed by a transition. It keeps its loop and leaves with its room.
  - In Chapter 1, the ball arrives with its room: it is already sitting at its home spot in the room and slides in with the room's pan, so it never rolls in separately or pops up. Other chapters decide room by room.
  - Every loop starts and ends on that pose. The ball may change scale between rooms; its stroke stays 1.5 at every scale (`transformAffectsStroke="false"`).
  - On the floor, the ball always sits on the floor line (y 712). It never travels down into the floor area.
- **Every intro stays exactly 3.2 s** in Rive, but the copy no longer waits for all of it (Demetre, 2026-11-03): on a room's first visit the copy arrives at the first-visit constant, 2.0 s after the scene is set, everywhere (community cards too); only a Chapter 2 game's first card with its lockup waits 3.2 s, so the copy never overlaps the lockup. A scene change inside one room (chapter-3 to chapter-3-2; ad, chapter-3-personal-badges and chapter-3-event-badges; chapter-4, chapter-4-top-game and chapter-4-editor-video; end to share; or the same scene again) is not a room change: no pan, and the copy waits 0.34 s (the quick swap). The two constants are `--yig-first-visit` and `--yig-lockup` in `engineering/yig-text-motion.css` (the plain-JS integrations read them from there) and `FIRST_VISIT_COPY_MS` and `LOCKUP_COPY_MS` in the React example's `timing.ts`. A room only plays its intro on its first visit; coming back (Back, or the Next after one) it slides in already built.
- **A move never reverses part-way.** Rive can't turn a room around mid-move without a snap (about a quarter of the move in one frame), so a Back during any pan, and a Next during a Back pan, wait in the integration file until the pan ends; only the last held tap counts. Next during a Next pan stays immediate.
- The game lockup (icon plus word) lives in Rive. It rises in after the pieces land and is gone exactly at 3.2 s. The small icon and the stats are HTML. The lockup uses the text's curves: expo.out in, power3.in out.
- **Community cards:** they appear in Chapter 2.5 (a player with exactly one qualifying game gets one for each other community game) and in the anon flow; a subscriber's game under 10 completed puzzles otherwise doesn't appear. The card tag stays `community: true` (a community card is a card type); in Rive the boolean is `lockup`, and a community card sets it to false, which hides only that game's Rive lockup (through its Lockup Gate); the intro, pan and backdrop are unchanged. The HTML icon still pops in with the card.
- Text motion (v3): Rise in 720 ms with a fade, 120 ms between lines; Lift out 240 ms, a short drift and dissolve, 20 ms apart, all clear within 340 ms (`--yig-clear`, which `yig-quick` waits and the integration file reads); Pop 560 ms. One mask per designed line. See `engineering/yig-text-motion.css`.
- Parked for later: Sink and stack, where the room sinks through the floor line, a color dome rises, and the pieces drop in.

## Scene loop guideline (Demetre, 2026-10-30)

This is the intent for every scene. **The details are set per scene, by Demetre, during the finesse round** (how the ball enters, whether it squashes, a full journey or a hop, where the loop starts); until then a scene keeps what it has, and only Chapter 1 card 1 follows the guideline now.

- **The ball's journey is the scene's loop**, and it keeps repeating for as long as the player stays on the scene.
- **The ball is revealed in a way that fits the room** (out of something, rolling in, dropping in), never just appearing.
- **The ball never visibly disappears.** It is only removed once it is out of sight, off the stage or hidden behind something.
- **The ball's loop reset is never visible.** Before it goes back to its start, it leaves into or behind an object and is masked there, clipping only the slot it passes through (as in Chapter 1's clock door and coffee mug). Where nothing can hide it, the loop ends exactly where it began, at the same position and speed.
- **The gap before the ball's journey starts again is set per scene.** There is no fixed value. (Card 1: 3 s.)
- **The room's small loops** (eyes, hands, pendulums, mice and so on) **keep running the whole time**, and their timing fits the full scene loop, with no jumps at the loop point.
- A ball must never wait where its room's exit would leave it on the stage: `npm run check` fails a loop that takes a piece beyond x 1585 (or before x -100), because the room pans 1600 away and the piece would float in the next room.

**The ball's role.** The ball is a continuous device, a quiet metaphor for the player's Year in Games, carried from room to room. Its job is to keep each frame alive while the viewer reads their stats, never to steal the show. It has weight and plays with the room it's in: the ball's journey starts as soon as the pieces it touches are in place. In rooms where those pieces ride in with the pan, that is the very start of the pan: the ball can already be moving as the room slides in. In rooms where they drop in during the intro, it starts once they've landed. This applies in Chapter 2 game rooms too, including while the lockup (the game's icon and name) is on screen, as in Wordle, where the ball rolls in during the lockup. If a lockup ever feels crowded, that room's finesse round decides it. It never waits for the copy, and it plays while the copy animates in; the ball keeps the frame alive without pulling focus. Pieces the ball touches react a little (a small dip, tilt or wobble that settles), in proportion to the impact, as in Wordle. Not every piece on every contact: choose the moments that add interest, and leave the rest still, so it never feels busy. The ball and its arcs never reach the copy. On the phone frame, keep the top of the ball's highest point well clear of the lowest line of the card's HTML copy. The details are set room by room.

**The ball rotates.** When it rolls, it turns in step with the distance it travels (one full turn per circumference), in the direction of travel: rolling right turns it clockwise, rolling left anticlockwise. In the air it keeps the spin it left the surface with, and a bounce can add a little. Once at rest it stops turning (see "Stillness" below for how it gets there). Its white dot travels around with the turn, which is what makes the motion read as rolling, not sliding.

**Stillness is a choice, not a default.** The ball never stops dead on a landing: it settles into rest, rolling on a touch and easing to a stop, sometimes with a tiny rock back, its dot turning with it. In pauses it usually keeps a faint, eased roll of a few pixels. Full stillness is used on purpose and sparingly, for drama (the gather before a big move, a held glance, the beat before a reveal); keep those holds short unless the moment earns more. Wordle (Chapter 2) and card 4 (Chapter 1) are the references.

**Squash and stretch stay subtle.** These are maximums, not targets: an anticipation gather of 5% over about 0.15 s, a landing squash of 12% springing back within 0.2 s with one tiny overshoot, a 3% stretch in flight on the fastest moves only, and 20% for a big, earned landing (Wordle's). They were raised on 2026-11-06 (from 6% and 8%) to match the rooms Demetre approved, measured in `docs/ball-character.md`: Wordle's landings squash 20 to 22%, card 4's and Chapter 3's about 9 to 10%. Volume stays constant (shorter means wider by the same amount). Each squash happens once and settles: never wobbly, never pulsing.

**The ball's physics.** One gravity: every flight in every room is a parabola (except the character jump, an eased arc: a fast launch, a floaty peak and an accelerating fall, `docs/ball-character.md`, "The jump", which `ball_speed.py` accepts as well) falling at the same rate, **0.40 artboard units per frame squared at 60 fps** (the existing landings measure 0.38 to 0.42 in Chapter 1 card 1, 0.42 to 0.50 in Wordle and 0.44 in Connections; a 30-frame fall drops 180 units). Contacts keep speed: a bounce or ricochet changes direction and keeps most of the speed (roughly 70 to 90 percent). A dead stop happens only on purpose, on a soft or catching surface such as landing in the mug. Only a surface can reverse the ball: it never changes direction in mid-air, and never off a surface that couldn't push it that way (a flat surface can't send a ball back the way it came). Slopes accelerate it: rolling downhill speeds up (by gravity times the sine of the slope), uphill slows down. A spring (a band, a seesaw) can return about the speed it received, not more, unless the piece is meant to fling, like Strands' strings: then a fling can give the ball more speed than it arrived with. Bounces off ordinary surfaces still keep 70 to 90 percent. The ball turns as it rolls (the rotation rule above). `python3 tools/ball_speed.py ch1 "<room>" --contacts` lists every contact of a room with the speed and direction in and out and flags what breaks these rules; run it on every room whose ball you change. The same check also looks for jitter, on the ball and on every piece the room moves (rotation, x, y): a **hitch** (stops for a frame or two in the middle of a movement and carries on), a **lurch or stall** (the speed jumps or collapses from one frame to the next with no contact to explain it) and a **buzz** (swings back and forth shorter than 20 frames, faster than about 3 a second, one after another without dying away). Stillness at a contact, at the end of a swing and at a real rest is fine. Fix every jitter flag before reporting a room. A jitter flag seen only in a later loop pass is first checked for a seam cause; if it is identical in every pass (the first pass is read across the intro-to-loop repeated frame), accept it as drawn. The check stays strict: the flags Demetre accepted on purpose (card 4's softer gravity, so far) are listed in `tools/ball_speed_accepted.json`, still reported but marked ACCEPTED instead of failing; adding one needs his approval. Wordle's and Connections' gravity flags (their intro roll-ins and hops) stay open until he says; he flags any fall that looks wrong.

**Illustrator imports.** Bring the illustrator's animation in faithfully, with our standard ball art and its dot turning with the travel.
- Fix without asking: a late start (start the loop as soon as the room's pieces are in place); seam mismatches and visible resets (match the last keys to the first); squash flicker (keep squash pulses 6 frames or longer); depth switches that pop (move the switch to a frame where the ball is clear); the ball crossing the copy (lower the arc); the ball resting visibly outside the phone frame (nudge it inside).
- Accept as drawn: the illustrator's physics, size changes, and exits hidden below the floor or outside the frame. Add them to `tools/ball_speed_accepted.json` with "as drawn" in the reason; this policy is his approval for those.
- Ask Demetre about anything else, or anything that changes the feel.

## State machine pattern (per chapter)

- Two enums, `scene` and `direction`, and a boolean, `lockup` (true shows the lockup). Three layers per room, plus a Lockup layer per game, so layers never key the same property:
  - `<Room>`: Waiting → Intro (scene == n, a scene name) → Loop
  - `<Room> Exit`: Home → Shown (scene == n) → Outro or Back Out (scene != n, by direction) → Gone or Right → Return or Forward In (scene == n) → Shown
  - `<Room> Draw`: Below ↔ On Top on scene == n
  - `<Room> Lockup` (games): Shown ↔ Hidden on lockup, read only while scene == n
- A `Backdrop` layer holds the colors: from AnyState, scene == n goes to `<Room> Colors` (a one-frame pose of the backdrop's wall and floor color) with a 1450 ms power1.inOut blend and early exit on. Rive mixes the two states over the blend, which is the crossfade; no other timeline keys the backdrop.
- The moves key only `<Room>` x and `<Room> Wall Lag` x; the draw rule is keyed only by the Draw layer's two poses.

## Build rules

- **A room's current state is the file; scripts are history.** A script that regenerates a whole room (or a whole file) says so in its docstring's first line and refuses to write a chapter's real `scene.rml` that has changed since the commit it was written against (`WRITTEN_AGAINST`, checked by `tools/regen_guard.py`; a copy under scratch is never refused, and `build.py` may also rewrite its own last build). To run one on purpose against a newer file, fold the changes into the script and move its `WRITTEN_AGAINST`. Guarded so far: `chapters/ch2/scripts/build.py` (the whole Chapter 2 file, and with it every Chapter 2 room script), `tools/ch1_cards_234.py`, `chapters/ch1/ch1_card_5.py`, `ch1_card_5_r3.py`, `ch1_start_idle.py`, `chapters/ch3/scripts/ch3_loop_pauses.py`, `ch3_c3s1_import.py`, `ch3_c4s1_import.py`, `ch3_c4_ball.py`, `ch3_c4_ball_stay.py`, `ch3_chapter3_loop.py`, `ch3_bird_on_ball.py` and `tools/import_room.py`. Chapter 4's building scripts (`ch3_c4_build_*.py`) already refuse when their layer exists.
- A room is whatever is in the Rive file now; there is no source version to match (see "Rooms"). A loop must start and end on one pose (the checks enforce it); a change may add a return or change the easing to get there. The ball's keys are plain direct keys (no joystick rig). `tools/compare_room.py` shows what a change did, against the last commit. The illustrator's file (`.cache/illustrator/`) is art only: never push to or edit it.
- **The Rive CLI is 1.9.1 since 2026-11-05** (1.6.0 stopped working). It is stricter: an empty artboard or shape fill is an error (every artboard `Fill` needs a `SolidColor`; a transparent `00000000` one changes nothing visually); `--once` is gone (`--publish` writes the .riv; `tools/package_engineering.py`, `tools/rive_size.py` and `harness/serve.mjs` use it); "interpolated-step" warnings (a linear key that jumps a whole value in one frame, which our loops do on purpose) are reported as warnings, and `tools/check.py` counts them but fails only on errors. Because the tags of the 2026-11-05 pushes still hold an empty Chapter 1 fill, `npm run package` (built from tags) stops on File 1 until Chapter 1 is pushed again. The "Chapter 3 Dim" check exception is gone. The push, the compare's pull and build, and the package build run the CLI through `tools/rive_cli.py`: a 3 minute limit, its own process group killed at the limit, no stdin and `RIVE_NO_TUI=1`, so a stuck CLI fails in 3 minutes instead of hanging; `tools/rive_push.py` exits 1 and prints "PUSH FAILED" on any failure or timeout. Run `rive_push.py` as a plain command: piped into `tail` or `grep`, the shell reports the pipe's exit code (0), not the push's.
- The Rive CLI is the primary tool. New artwork goes in through the Rive Editor first (the CLI has no SVG import), then `rive pull`.
- Wrap anything that rotates or squashes so its group's own position sits on the pivot, with an inner "Art" group offset back.
- Keep `includeInExport` true on every artboard that ships.
- Never edit in the Editor and locally at the same time. Pull before local edits, verify and screenshot before pushing, and push with a name through `tools/rive_push.py` (see "Pushing on your own"), after `tools/rive_compare.py`; an Editor edit it finds is pulled in and committed first. Commit to git at every named push.
- When changing timing, keep each intro at 3.2 s, and keep each loop's first and last frames identical.
- **The test commands** (when each runs is in "How we work now"): `npm run finesse <ch> "<room>"` runs the chapter's rule checks, that room's ball check, and prints the `/experience` address that opens on it. `npm run smoke` runs only when the reference code, the harness, the guide or the runner changed, and says why it ran or skipped; both smoke and `npm run suite` skip a chapter whose file and tools are unchanged since that piece last passed (`-- --all` runs everything).
  Smoke and the suite run under `caffeinate -i` (the Mac can't sleep), start the review server if it isn't running, give each piece a 15 minute limit, kill the headless Chrome and package web server they started before each piece, stop at the first failure and say which piece, why and where its log is (`test-logs/`, git-ignored, overwritten each run). A browser test that loses Chrome fails with "Chrome stopped: ..." instead of hanging; read that message before re-running, and don't retry blindly. Don't run the tests by hand piece by piece unless you are chasing one failure.
  The Chapter 2 page and the text test meet only at one entry point, `window.__yigTest = { player, cards? }` (see `harness/index.html`): the test gives the page the stats and, for timing cases, the exact card list. No other hook in a demo page may be read by a test.
- After every change to a Rive file: run `--verify` and the automatic rule checks, as one script that takes about a second: `npm run check <ch>`. It checks that Waiting poses equal their intro's first keys, every intro is exactly 3.2 s, every loop's first and last frames match (and start on the intro's last frame), everything clears the artboard, and no moving piece strays beyond the room's bounds. Don't re-render, pixel-diff or time anything the change didn't touch. Full render, pixel and timing passes (`npm run tap`, `npm run back`, `npm run text`) are part of `npm run suite`, run once at the final delivery or when Demetre asks. Motion and feel are reviewed by Demetre in `/experience` (with the Finesse panel), so when a change affects what's on screen, say exactly what to look at.
- Rive draws a group's first child on top, so children are drawn in reverse of the order they are written. Anything that must sit behind everything, such as the shared backdrop, is the last child.
- A color change is a blend between two hold poses, set by the transition's duration and ease; never author the in-between, and never give a room its own backdrop. Turn on early exit, or a tap mid-fade waits for the old fade to finish and the color jumps.
- RML omits `value="0"` on keys, so read keys with a real XML parser and treat a missing value as 0. A regular expression reads them as missing, and that once put shadows and lockups at full size in a Waiting pose.
- Each Waiting pose must equal its intro's first keys. `npm run check` tests this for every room; after editing an intro, rebuild its Waiting pose from the intro, never by hand.
- A leaving room draws below the arriving room through draw rules, not by reordering rooms: each room's Draw layer puts its rule on Top (the shared Top Marker, above every room) while it is the scene and on Bottom otherwise. A draw target has to name a Shape, not a group.
- Each lockup lives inside its own room so it leaves with the room.
- To test taps at exact moments, put test-only listeners in a throwaway copy and press them with `--pointer=down` (see `tools/back_test.py`); `--data` only sets values before the run starts. Binding a node's x out to the view model (`direction="true"`) with `--data-dump-every=1` records where it is on every frame.
